## Germany

The gap between Poland and Germany is usually the largest of all its neighbours, because Germany has high excise duty and a CO₂ levy on fuel. That's why stations on the Polish side at Słubice, Zgorzelec or Kołbaskowo are busy with German drivers. Heading west, fill up before the border; heading east, wait until you're in Poland if your range allows.

On the [Szczecin – Berlin](route/szczecin-berlin) drive, for example, almost the whole trip is in Germany – the calculator costs that part at the German price.

## Czechia and Slovakia

The differences here are smaller and change from week to week – sometimes Poland is cheaper, sometimes the neighbour. Check the table before you go. Czech and Slovak motorways need an electronic vignette (edalnice.cz, eznamka.sk), so add its price to the fuel cost.

## Lithuania

On the [Warsaw – Vilnius](route/warsaw-vilnius) route, nearly 40% of the distance is in Lithuania. Compare the prices in the table: if the Lithuanian side is dearer, fill up around Suwałki or Augustów.

## Things to watch out for

- **Stations right at the border** can charge more than the national average precisely because customers come from the dearer country. A few kilometres further in, it's often cheaper.
- **Motorway stations** are almost always above average, on both sides of the border.
- **Don't drive out of your way for it.** A 20 km round-trip detour at 7 l/100 km burns nearly 3 litres, which wipes out a small price difference.
