On a longer drive you usually combine both: you set off with a battery charged at home and top up along the way. The real cost lands somewhere between the two EV columns.

## What drives electricity use

- **Speed** – at motorway speeds an EV uses noticeably more than in town. It's the opposite of a combustion car, which burns the most in traffic jams – in town an EV recovers energy when braking.
- **Temperature** – in winter the battery and cabin heating raise consumption, by up to several tens of percent. Pre-heating the car while it's still plugged in helps.
- **Car size** – small city cars use around 14–15 kWh/100 km, large SUVs over 20 kWh.

> With a night tariff or your own solar panels, home charging can cost even less than in the table. Pricey motorway chargers cost more.

## When an EV pays off

You save the most when most of your kilometres run on electricity from home or work, and you only use fast chargers on longer trips. If you can't charge day to day and rely only on fast chargers, the running cost can come close to a diesel's.
