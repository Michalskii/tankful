## How to work it out yourself

Cost of 100 km = consumption (l/100 km) × price per litre.

For example, a car using 7 l/100 km at {{price DE pb}} spends {{cost100 DE pb 7}} per 100 km. For any distance, multiply that by the number of kilometres and divide by 100. If you think in miles per gallon, divide 282.5 by your UK mpg (or 235.2 by US mpg) to get litres per 100 km. More examples in [How to calculate the fuel cost of a trip](guides/how-to-calculate-fuel-cost).

## Which car is cheapest to run

- **LPG** is usually the cheapest fuel per kilometre, even though a car on gas burns 15–30% more than on petrol. You do have to pay for the conversion and its servicing.
- **Diesel** uses less than petrol, so it's often cheaper per kilometre even when a litre costs more. The advantage grows on long trips.
- **An EV** charged at home is the cheapest of all. On a road trip using fast chargers, the cost approaches petrol and can exceed it.

## What your real consumption depends on

The brochure figure comes from a test cycle; in practice you'll usually use more. Speed matters most: driving at 140 km/h instead of 110 km/h can raise consumption by 20–30%. Load, a roof box, air conditioning, winter and traffic jams add to it. If you know your car's consumption from the trip computer, enter it in the [calculator](en) – the result will be more accurate than with typical values.
