## Where to refuel on the way to Denmark

From Poland the usual route to Denmark crosses Germany – on the A7 via Flensburg or to the Rostock – Gedser ferry.

- **Fill up in Poland** before entering Germany – from Szczecin a full tank (about 50 l, roughly 700 km) usually reaches southern Denmark, and from Poznań at least the Danish border.
- If you need fuel in Germany, use ordinary stations or Autohof truck stops at the exits rather than motorway service areas.
- On the way back, compare Denmark with Germany in the table above – if Germany is cheaper, buy only enough to reach the first German station after the border.

Over 700 km through Germany and Denmark, a car using 7 l/100 km spends about {{cost PL pb 7 700}} at Polish prices, {{cost DE pb 7 700}} at German prices and {{cost DK pb 7 700}} at Danish prices.

## Fuel names and paying

> In Denmark 95 petrol is **Blyfri 95** and diesel is **Diesel**. LPG is hard to find in Denmark – if your car runs on gas, fill up before the border. You pay by card, and many stations are unstaffed and open around the clock. If the terminal asks about currency, choose **kroner** – your bank's conversion is usually cheaper.

The Great Belt (Storebælt) bridge and the Øresund bridge to Sweden are tolled separately. Cost your own trip to Denmark in the [Tankful calculator](en), and compare every EU country in the [fuel prices in Europe](guides/fuel-prices-europe) table.
