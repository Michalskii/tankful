## Why you use more LPG

LPG holds less energy per litre than petrol, so the engine burns more of it by volume – usually 15–25% more. The car also starts on petrol and switches to gas once warm, so on short trips part of the fuel is still petrol. The figures above assume about 20% more LPG than petrol.

## Who LPG makes sense for

- **High-mileage drivers** – fifteen thousand kilometres a year or more. The system then pays off within a year or two.
- **Cars with large petrol engines** – the higher the consumption, the bigger the saving on every kilometre.
- **Long-distance drivers** – on long stretches you're on gas almost the whole time.

LPG pays off less if you drive little, mostly short trips in town, or if your engine has direct injection – systems for those engines cost noticeably more.

## Things to keep in mind

- **Running costs:** the system needs servicing and filter changes, and in Poland an LPG car needs a roadworthiness test every year.
- **Boot space:** the round tank usually takes the place of the spare wheel.
- **Car parks:** some underground car parks don't admit LPG cars.
- **Abroad:** LPG is available in most of Europe, but not everywhere as widely as in Poland, and filling up can require an adapter. Check LPG prices in other countries in the [fuel prices in Europe](guides/fuel-prices-europe) table.
