The fuel cost of a trip comes down to one formula:

> **cost = distance (km) ÷ 100 × consumption (l/100 km) × price per litre**

For example, the [Warsaw – Berlin](route/warsaw-berlin) drive is {{km warszawa-berlin}} km. A car using 7 l/100 km burns {{litres warszawa-berlin 7}} litres of petrol on it, which at the route's weighted average price of {{triprate warszawa-berlin pb}} comes to **{{trip warszawa-berlin pb 7}}** one way.

## Step by step

1. **Check the distance** of the route you'll actually drive – in your navigation app or the calculator. The shortest route isn't always the fastest, and the difference can be tens of kilometres.
2. **Use your real consumption**, ideally from the trip computer on a motorway drive. City and motorway figures differ a lot.
3. **Multiply the distance by the consumption and divide by 100** – that's the number of litres.
4. **Multiply the litres by the price per litre.** On a trip through several countries, cost each section at that country's price.
5. **For a return trip** double the result; if several people share the car, divide by the number of people.

If you work in miles and mpg: litres = miles × 1.609 ÷ 100 × (282.5 ÷ UK mpg). Or skip the maths and let the calculator switch to miles and gallons.

On international routes the price is weighted by the kilometres in each country – so Warsaw – Paris, for example, is costed largely at German prices.

## A trip through several countries

If a route crosses several countries, a single price gives the wrong answer. It's more accurate to split the route into sections: kilometres in each country × consumption ÷ 100 × the price in that country, then add them up. For Warsaw – Amsterdam, {{km warszawa-amsterdam}} km across three countries, the kilometre-weighted petrol price is {{triprate warszawa-amsterdam pb}}.

## The most common mistakes

- **Using the brochure consumption instead of the real one.** Manufacturers quote a test result – on the motorway you'll usually use 10–30% more.
- **Forgetting the way back** or driving around at the destination.
- **One price for a whole international trip**, although fuel next door can be 30 cents a litre dearer or cheaper.
- **Counting a ferry as road.** A car on a ferry burns no fuel – the calculator leaves ferry sections out, but it's easy to forget when you do it by hand.
