## How to pay less for fuel in Germany

- **Fill up in Poland before the border.** At today's prices you save {{tank PL DE pb 50}} on 50 litres of petrol. On shorter routes such as Szczecin – Berlin, a tank from Poland covers the return trip too.
- **Fill up in the evening.** German stations change their prices many times a day. Mornings tend to be the most expensive and evenings the cheapest.
- **Check prices in an app.** German stations must report every price change to the Markttransparenzstelle für Kraftstoffe, the federal fuel price transparency unit. Free apps and sites such as clever-tanken.de and mehr-tanken.de use that data.
- **Leave the motorway.** A station in the next town is often several tens of cents per litre cheaper than a Raststätte.

> In Germany, 95 petrol is sold as **Super E5** and the cheaper **Super E10** (up to 10% ethanol). Most cars built after 2010 can run on E10 – check the manual or the inside of the fuel flap. Diesel is **Diesel** and LPG is **Autogas**.

## Road charges in Germany

Cars don't pay to use German motorways – there's no vignette and no toll booths. Watch out for low emission zones (Umweltzone) in many cities, including Berlin and Munich: driving in requires a green emissions sticker, best ordered before the trip.
