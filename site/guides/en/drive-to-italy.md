From Kraków or Wrocław to Lake Garda or Venice it is roughly 1,100–1,300 km, from Warsaw even more. For 1,300 km in a car that uses 7 l/100 km, petrol costs about **{{cost PL pb 7 1300}}** at Polish prices and about **{{cost IT pb 7 1300}}** at Italian prices – one way. A diesel at 6 l/100 km uses about {{cost PL on 6 1300}} worth of fuel at Polish prices. With four people in the car, petrol comes to about {{cost PL pb 1.75 1300}} each.

## Where to fill up on the way

- **Poland or Czechia:** {{verdict CZ PL pb}} Fill up in whichever is cheaper.
- **Austria or Italy:** {{verdict IT AT pb}} {{verdict IT AT on}} If Austria is cheaper, fill up before the pass.
- **In Italy**, for short trips around the country fill up away from the motorway – motorway stations are usually above the average.
- **On the way back**, if fuel is dearer in Italy, it pays to reach Austria or Poland with just enough in the tank.

## Vignettes and tolls

Czech and Austrian motorways need a vignette, while Italian motorways charge per section at toll booths. Some sections and tunnels have separate tolls on top. Check the current rules and rates on the official websites before you go – here we only count fuel.

## How much your car really uses on a trip like this

Long motorway driving with a full boot and a roof box can raise consumption by more than ten percent over the brochure figure. If your car normally uses 7 l/100 km, on 1,300 km with luggage plan for about 8 l/100 km – that is about {{cost PL pb 8 1300}} of petrol at Polish prices.
