## 9 ways to use less fuel

1. **Slow down on the motorway.** Air resistance grows with the square of speed, so 120 km/h instead of 140 km/h typically saves well over ten percent of fuel. On a long holiday drive that's anything from a few litres to well over ten, and the trip takes less extra time than you'd think.
2. **Drive smoothly.** Hard acceleration and braking turn fuel into brake heat. Look far ahead and lift off the accelerator early.
3. **Use engine braking.** When you coast in gear with your foot off the accelerator, a modern fuel-injected engine uses no fuel at all. In neutral it has to idle, which does use fuel.
4. **Change up earlier.** Lower revs in a higher gear use less fuel – as long as the engine isn't labouring.
5. **Check your tyre pressure.** Under-inflated tyres raise rolling resistance and fuel use, and wear out faster. Check them monthly and before a long trip, and when fully loaded use the full-load pressure shown on the door pillar or fuel flap.
6. **Take the roof box off when you're not using it.** A roof box or bars can raise consumption by well over ten percent at motorway speeds. The same luggage inside the car weighs the same but adds no drag.
7. **Don't carry what you don't need.** Extra weight costs fuel when pulling away and climbing hills, especially in town.
8. **Use air conditioning sensibly.** Air conditioning increases consumption, most of all in town. At motorway speeds open windows add drag too, so there the air conditioning is usually the better choice.
9. **Avoid short trips on a cold engine.** A cold engine uses the most fuel, and on a 2–3 km trip it never warms up. Combining errands into one trip uses less fuel than separate journeys.

> Motorway speed and smooth driving make the biggest difference. The other tips save a few percent each, but together they can cut consumption by a litre or more.
