- **2008** – Brent crude passed 140 dollars a barrel in the summer before falling sharply in the financial crisis.
- **2011–2013** – oil above 100 dollars a barrel kept fuel expensive for three years in a row.
- **2015–2016** – the oil price collapse brought pump prices back to 2005–2010 levels.
- **2020** – the pandemic cut demand and prices for a few months.
- **2022** – the war in Ukraine pushed prices to records; diesel was dearer than petrol for months.

## What drives fuel prices

- **Crude oil prices** on world markets – the largest single part of the cost of fuel before tax.
- **Exchange rates** – oil is priced in US dollars, so a weaker local currency raises pump prices even when oil gets cheaper.
- **Taxes** – excise duty and VAT usually make up around half of the price of a litre in the EU. Governments sometimes cut them temporarily: in 2022, for example, Poland lowered VAT on fuel from 23% to 8% from February to the end of the year, which softened the price spike.
- **Wholesale and retail margins** – refiners, importers and station operators each add their own margin.
